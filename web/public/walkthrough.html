Reflective Lens

A walkthrough for coaches

One journey, for the coach. You capture your thoughts in your own words, by speaking or typing, answer a gentle prompt or two for a little more, and see it reflected back. Notes, reflections and answers can all be a voice note or text. Nothing is ever graded. Players appear as members of your squad: they have no accounts, they are never contacted, and only their first names are used in anything written back to you.

A mirror, not a verdict

Coach From a session to a reflection

Set your intent and pick your squad, capture notes as they happen by voice or text, record the result, then reflect. The app only asks for a little more where a note is thin, and always in your own language.

Output What the reports look like

This is the payoff. Every report restates only what you and your notes actually said. It reviews what you hoped to see, honestly, and reflects your own words back. Nothing is invented, nothing is graded. The examples below are illustrative.

Coach, match report

U12 Lions vs Ashwood

You wanted brave build-up and calmer heads under pressure. You wrote most about the build-up. You did not come back to the pressure.
Result: won 3-1, at home. Saturday 12 July.

What you hoped to see

  • · Keeper starts the moves (you wrote about this). Noted twice, and calmly.
  • · Centre-backs split wide (your notes touch on this). There in the first half.
  • · Calm under pressure (nothing in your notes about this one).

What went well

  • Two of the three goals started with the keeper playing short.

What got in the way

  • You said the panic came when Ashwood pressed with two, not one.

Noted for next

  • You said you would rehearse the press-release.
Drawn from your notes and your reflection. Export as a PDF to share.
Coach, monthly report

U12 Lions, training in July

Four training sessions reflected on. The build-up keeps coming up in your notes. So does the last twenty minutes.
Training only. Matchdays are reported separately.

What keeps appearing

  • You mentioned playing out from the back in three of the four sessions.
  • You twice wrote that concentration dropped near the end.

What you said changed

  • You wrote that the keeper is starting moves without being told to.

Training and matchday

  • The build-up you have worked on in training is what you wrote most about on Saturday.

Looking back over the month

  • What would you change or improve, if anything?
Drawn only from what you wrote. Kept to one team and one kind of session, because a goalkeeping session is not the team's training picture.
One principle, all the way through. You capture your thoughts in your own words, answer a gentle prompt for a little more, and see it reflected back rather than judged. Grounded in England Football coaching practice, and always written in your voice.
